# The shelf behind this book

*A working draft, November 2026. This book is being written while The Grove is being built; expect it to change. Checked against the build as it stood on 1 November 2026.*

---

Some of the books behind this book are in the notes to each chapter. Others shaped the thinking from further back, long before there was code, and don't fit neatly into any one chapter's footnotes. They're here, grouped loosely, with a line on why each matters and where its influence shows. It's a shelf, not a syllabus.

## Organising without a boss

- **Laloux, F. (2014), *Reinventing Organizations*, Nelson Parker.** The book that showed whole organisations already working this way. Its threads of self-management, wholeness and evolutionary purpose run through Parts two and three.
- **Bockelbrink, B., Priest, J. and David, L., *Sociocracy 3.0: A Practical Guide*, freely available from the authors.** A modular, openly licensed take on sociocracy: patterns any group can pick up, including consent and the drivers behind a change. Its spirit of small, separable practices sits close to Chapter 10.
- **Robertson, B. J. (2015), *Holacracy*, Henry Holt.** Roles over people, tensions as fuel, a written constitution. Much of the structure in Chapters 8 and 10 began as a conversation with it.
- **Nixon, T. (2021), *Work with Source*.** Who brings something into the world, and how responsibility is taken rather than given. Chapters 6 and 17 agree with it and part company with it.

## Nature, self-organisation and software

- **Di Marzo Serugendo, G., Karageorgos, A., Rana, O. F. and Zambonelli, F. (eds) (2004), *Engineering Self-Organising Systems: Nature-Inspired Approaches to Software Engineering*, Springer.**
- **Di Marzo Serugendo, G., Gleizes, M.-P. and Karageorgos, A. (eds) (2011), *Self-organising Software: From Natural to Artificial Adaptation*, Springer.** Two collections on building software that coordinates the way colonies and swarms do, without a central controller. They sit behind the idea, in Chapters 3 and 5, that the rules for coordination can live in the system itself.
- **Fukuoka, M. (1978), *The One-Straw Revolution*, Rodale Press.** A farmer who learned to do less and let the land do more. A quiet companion to Chapter 3's argument that much of what we impose, nature already does.

## Economy, money and the commons

- **Raworth, K. (2017), *Doughnut Economics: Seven Ways to Think Like a 21st-Century Economist*, Random House Business.** An economy that meets everyone's needs within the planet's limits, with a social floor beneath and an ecological ceiling above. It sits behind the floor in Chapter 15 and the society picture in Chapter 25.
- **Ostrom, E. (1990), *Governing the Commons*, Cambridge University Press.** People governing shared resources well without a ruler or an owner. Chapters 1 and 15.

## What people could become

- **Hillman, J. (1996), *The Soul's Code: In Search of Character and Calling*, Random House.** The idea that each person carries a calling. It sits behind seats chosen rather than assigned, in Chapter 6, and behind members saying, in their own words, which open seat they'd like, in Chapter 9.
- **Eisenstein, C. (2007), *The Ascent of Humanity*, Panenthea Press.** A long argument that our crises grow from a story of separation, and that a story of connection is possible. Chapters 1 and 25.
- **Macy, J. and Brown, M. Y. (2014), *Coming Back to Life: The Updated Guide to the Work That Reconnects*, New Society Publishers.** Turning grief for the world into the energy to act, together. The tone of Chapter 25 owes it something.
- **Vaughan-Lee, L. (ed.) (2013), *Spiritual Ecology: The Cry of the Earth*, The Golden Sufi Center.** Essays on the inner side of the ecological crisis: that how we see the world shapes how we treat it.
- **Hubbard, B. M. (1998), *Conscious Evolution: Awakening the Power of Our Social Potential*, New World Library.** The social potential movement: the question of human potential asked of people together. Chapters 1 and 25.
- **Gladwell, M. (2005), *Blink: The Power of Thinking Without Thinking*, Little, Brown.** How much of what we know, we know before we can explain it. A small piece of the case in Chapter 25 that thinking isn't our only intelligence.

## AI and the future

- **Bostrom, N. (2014), *Superintelligence: Paths, Dangers, Strategies*, Oxford University Press.** The serious case for caution about capable AI. Part of why, in Chapter 18, Wise-Eye's limits are held in structure rather than in instructions.
- **Harari, Y. N. (2018), *21 Lessons for the 21st Century*, Jonathan Cape.** What work, meaning and freedom become as algorithms take more of each. Chapter 25 answers some of its questions differently.
- **Davidson, J. D. and Rees-Mogg, W. (1997), *The Sovereign Individual*, Simon and Schuster.** A very different use of the word *sovereign*, built on the individual rather than the relationship. Chapter 1 explains why this book doesn't share it.

If a book shaped how you think about any of this, and you think it belongs here, tell us.
